Intervals are not morality — they are risk management. A Datejust that sees desk duty and occasional weekends behaves differently than a Submariner that lives in salt air. Roughly **60–70%** of the "should I wait?" conversations we have in the Lehigh Valley come down to environment and shock history, not calendar age alone.

## What Does Rolex Officially Recommend?

Rolex's modern positioning emphasizes long intervals for recently manufactured watches, reflecting improvements in oils, alloys, and manufacturing consistency. That guidance assumes "normal" wear — not ignored water exposure, not magnetism from laptops, and not years of ignored amplitude loss.

## When Do Independents Suggest Service Sooner Than a Decade?

Many working watchmakers still use a **5–7 year** mental model for automatics that run daily — not because they want work, but because lubricants age and wear accelerates once friction rises. If your watch suddenly gains or loses time in multiple positions, that is data — not a fluke.

## What Signals Mean You Should Move Service Up?

Crown grit, date snap hesitation, poor power reserve versus factory spec, and obvious magnetism after travel are all reasons to stop waiting. A timing machine trace can show problems while the watch still "looks fine" on the wrist — which is why evaluations beat guessing.
