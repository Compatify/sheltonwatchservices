Stoppage is a symptom, not a diagnosis. On quartz, a **dead cell accounts for a large share** of sudden stops — but not all. On mechanicals, a watch that "used to wind fine" but now resists can signal broken automatic winding components or deeper train issues.

## What Should You Check First on a Quartz Watch?

A fresh cell from a competent installer is the baseline. If a new battery does not restore a tick, suspect coil, gear, or contamination issues — pushing harder on the crown rarely helps and can damage setting levers.

## What Should You Check First on a Mechanical Watch?

Hand-wind if the movement supports it; auto winders are not a substitute for understanding power reserve. If the balance refuses to swing after reasonable winding, stop — that is bench territory.

## When Is It Time to Stop Troubleshooting at Home?

Magnetism can masquerade as a dead watch; so can dried oils. If quick checks fail, a watchmaker can measure amplitude, lift angle behavior, and keyless action in minutes — saving you from buying parts you do not need.
